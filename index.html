Method and definitions

How we counted and tested

What we read
Source
Every public comment and reply on the listed posts, collected to 26 October 2026, with one set of labels per comment.
Labels
Each comment gets one target (the creator, the post content, Meta or the glasses, another brand, the sponsorship, other commenters), one sentiment toward that target, a harshness level (none, criticism, hostility) and a theme. Labels come from an AI classifier built for this study.
Readable comments
The base for every rate. Excludes spam and giveaway entries (including any comment with a baby-registry, fundraiser or payment link), comments with no readable subject, empty comments, and the creator's own comments.
Negative about Meta
Negative comments whose target is Meta, its leadership, the glasses or the deal itself (“sellout”, “how much did they pay you”), as a share of readable comments. The deal is counted with Meta because on a Meta post, criticism of the deal is a reaction to the same partnership. Every “about Meta” figure in this readout uses this definition.
Negative about the creator
Negative comments whose target is the creator as a person, as a share of readable comments.
Hostile
Insults, slurs, degrading or sexual remarks, harassment or threats aimed at a person. Harsh criticism of an idea or product is not hostility.
Mentions Meta
The comment contains words that point to Meta or the glasses: names, nicknames, pronouns that refer to them, their features, or concerns that only apply to them.
Weighting
Every figure counts each creator equally: we work out each creator's rate, then average across creators with at least 30 readable comments. This stops a few very large comment sections, such as one viral post, from deciding the result.
Groups
Gen Z: the 10 contracted Gen Z campaign creators. Paid posts: the 10 paid deliverables; all Meta posts adds their earned glasses posts. Other creators: US Meta Glasses paid posts from non-Gen Z creators in the same window, plus 1M+ creators from June to August. Everyday posts: each creator's non-sponsored posts near their Meta posts. Tier 2 and Tier 3: paid posts from US Tier 2 Ambassadors and Tier 3 Creators in the Meta Glasses programme.
How we tested

Four tests, one bar: 95% confidence

  • Creators are the unit, not comments. Comments on one creator's posts are not independent: one viral post can add thousands of comments that all react to the same thing. So every test compares creators, and a creator counts only with at least 30 readable comments.
  • One bar for every test. Stat sig means a gap this large would appear by chance less than 1 time in 20 if there were no real difference (95% confidence). Not stat sig means chance can't be ruled out. Every test looks for a gap in either direction.
  • Tests by reshuffling. With 10 Gen Z creators and a few extreme values, standard formulas that assume a bell curve can mislead. Instead each test reshuffles the data thousands of times to see what gaps chance alone produces, then checks whether the real gap is bigger than 95% of them (a permutation test).
  • Each 95% range comes from the same test. It holds every gap the test can't rule out, so a result is stat sig exactly when its range excludes zero.
  • What is not tested. Tier 2 and Tier 3 figures and the comment examples are descriptive. They carry no stat sig label.
